What is unsupervised learning and how does it differ from supervised learning?	Unsupervised learning discovers hidden patterns in unlabeled data without predefined target variables. Unlike supervised learning (which predicts labels), unsupervised learning finds structure, groups similar items, reduces dimensions, and uncovers associations without knowing the "right answer".<br><br>Key differences:<br>• <b>Supervised</b>: Has labeled data (X→y), predicts outcomes<br>• <b>Unsupervised</b>: No labels, discovers patterns<br><br>Common tasks: Clustering, PCA, Association Rules, Recommendations
What is K-Means clustering and what are its key assumptions?	K-Means partitions data into K spherical clusters by minimizing within-cluster variance (WCSS/inertia).<br><br><b>Algorithm:</b><br>1. Initialize K random centroids<br>2. Assign points to nearest centroid<br>3. Recalculate centroids as cluster means<br>4. Repeat until convergence<br><br><b>Assumptions:</b><br>• Spherical clusters<br>• Similar cluster sizes<br>• Known K value<br>• Linear separability<br><br><b>Sensitive to:</b> Initial centroids, outliers, scaling
What is the Silhouette Score and how do you interpret it?	Silhouette Score measures how well points fit their assigned clusters.<br><br><b>Formula:</b> s = (b - a) / max(a, b)<br>• a = average intra-cluster distance<br>• b = average nearest-cluster distance<br><br><b>Interpretation:</b><br>• Range: [-1, 1]<br>• > 0.7: Strong structure<br>• 0.5-0.7: Reasonable structure<br>• 0.25-0.5: Weak structure<br>• < 0.25: No structure<br>• Negative: Wrong cluster assignment<br><br>Higher is better - indicates good cohesion and separation
What is WCSS (Within-Cluster Sum of Squares) and how is it used?	WCSS (also called inertia) is the sum of squared distances from points to their cluster centroids.<br><br><b>Formula:</b> WCSS = Σ Σ ||x - μᵢ||²<br><br><b>Properties:</b><br>• Always decreases with more clusters<br>• K=n gives WCSS=0 (overfitting)<br>• Lower is better for same K<br><br><b>Elbow Method:</b><br>• Plot WCSS vs K<br>• Look for "elbow" where decrease slows<br>• Balance between low WCSS and few clusters<br><br><b>Use for:</b> Choosing optimal K, ranking cluster quality
What are the different linkage methods in Hierarchical Clustering?	<b>Single Linkage:</b><br>• Distance = minimum between any two points<br>• Creates elongated "chains"<br>• Sensitive to noise/outliers<br><br><b>Complete Linkage:</b><br>• Distance = maximum between any two points<br>• Creates compact, spherical clusters<br>• Robust to outliers<br><br><b>Average Linkage:</b><br>• Distance = average of all pairs<br>• Balanced approach<br><br><b>Ward's Method:</b><br>• Minimizes within-cluster variance increase<br>• Creates equally-sized clusters<br>• Often gives best results<br>• Most similar to K-means
How do you read and interpret a dendrogram?	A dendrogram is a tree diagram showing hierarchical clustering relationships.<br><br><b>Reading:</b><br>• X-axis: Data points or clusters<br>• Y-axis: Distance/dissimilarity at merge<br>• Horizontal lines: Cluster merges<br>• Vertical lines: Clusters being merged<br><br><b>Interpretation:</b><br>• Height = merge distance<br>• Large jumps = natural cluster boundaries<br>• Cut horizontally to get K clusters<br>• Earlier merges = more similar<br>• Later merges = more different<br><br><b>Choosing K:</b> Look for large vertical gaps
What is DBSCAN and when should you use it?	DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) groups points based on density.<br><br><b>Parameters:</b><br>• ε (epsilon): Neighborhood radius<br>• MinPts: Minimum points for dense region<br><br><b>Point Types:</b><br>• <b>Core:</b> Has ≥ MinPts within ε<br>• <b>Border:</b> Within ε of core point<br>• <b>Noise:</b> Neither core nor border (-1)<br><br><b>When to use:</b><br>• Arbitrary cluster shapes<br>• Unknown number of clusters<br>• Presence of outliers<br>• Varying densities<br><br><b>Advantages:</b> No K needed, handles noise, finds non-spherical clusters
What is PCA and what problem does it solve?	PCA (Principal Component Analysis) finds new axes (principal components) that capture maximum variance in data.<br><br><b>Problems it solves:</b><br>• Curse of dimensionality<br>• Multicollinearity<br>• Visualization of high-D data<br>• Noise reduction<br>• Feature extraction<br><br><b>How it works:</b><br>1. Standardize data<br>2. Compute covariance matrix<br>3. Find eigenvectors (directions) & eigenvalues (variance)<br>4. Order by variance explained<br>5. Keep top components<br><br><b>Key property:</b> Components are orthogonal (uncorrelated)
How do you determine the optimal number of principal components?	<b>Methods to choose components:</b><br><br><b>1. Cumulative Variance:</b><br>• Keep components explaining 90-95% variance<br>• Plot cumulative variance vs components<br>• Common threshold: 90%<br><br><b>2. Scree Plot:</b><br>• Plot eigenvalues vs component number<br>• Look for "elbow" where curve flattens<br>• Keep components before elbow<br><br><b>3. Kaiser Criterion:</b><br>• Keep components with eigenvalue > 1<br>• (More variance than single variable)<br><br><b>Rule of thumb:</b> If 2-3 components explain 90%, huge reduction achieved
What are eigenvalues and eigenvectors in PCA?	<b>Eigenvectors:</b><br>• Directions of maximum variance<br>• New axes for transformed space<br>• Orthogonal to each other<br>• Linear combinations of original features<br><br><b>Eigenvalues:</b><br>• Amount of variance in each direction<br>• Larger = more important component<br>• Sum equals total variance<br>• Used to calculate % variance explained<br><br><b>Relationship:</b><br>• PC1 has largest eigenvalue<br>• Eigenvalue = variance along eigenvector<br>• Eigenvector shows feature contributions
What is Content-Based Filtering and how does it work?	Content-Based Filtering recommends items similar to what user liked before, based on item features.<br><br><b>Process:</b><br>1. Extract item features (genre, director, keywords)<br>2. Build user profile (average of liked items)<br>3. Calculate similarity (cosine, Euclidean)<br>4. Recommend most similar items<br><br><b>Advantages:</b><br>• No cold-start for items<br>• Transparent reasoning<br>• Works for unique tastes<br>• No need for other users<br><br><b>Limitations:</b><br>• Overspecialization (filter bubble)<br>• Feature engineering needed<br>• No serendipity
What is Collaborative Filtering and how does it differ from Content-Based?	Collaborative Filtering uses patterns from similar users' behaviors to make recommendations.<br><br><b>Content-Based vs Collaborative:</b><br>• <b>Content:</b> "Items similar to what you liked"<br>• <b>Collaborative:</b> "What similar users also liked"<br><br><b>Types:</b><br>• <b>User-based:</b> Find similar users → their items<br>• <b>Item-based:</b> Find similar items by co-ratings<br>• <b>Matrix Factorization:</b> SVD, NMF<br><br><b>Advantages:</b> Serendipity, no features needed<br><b>Challenges:</b> Cold-start, sparsity, scalability
What is SVD in recommendation systems?	SVD (Singular Value Decomposition) factorizes user-item matrix into latent factors.<br><br><b>How it works:</b><br>• R ≈ U × Σ × V^T<br>• U: User factors<br>• V: Item factors<br>• Σ: Importance of factors<br><br><b>Parameters:</b><br>• n_factors: Latent dimensions (typically 50-200)<br>• n_epochs: Training iterations<br>• Learning rate & regularization<br><br><b>Advantages:</b><br>• Handles sparsity well<br>• Captures latent patterns<br>• Better than KNN methods<br>• Scalable
How do you evaluate recommendation systems?	<b>Common Metrics:</b><br><br><b>RMSE (Root Mean Square Error):</b><br>• √(Σ(actual - predicted)²/n)<br>• < 1.0: Excellent for 5-point scale<br>• 1.0-1.5: Good performance<br><br><b>MAE (Mean Absolute Error):</b><br>• Σ|actual - predicted|/n<br>• More interpretable than RMSE<br>• Less sensitive to outliers<br><br><b>Other metrics:</b><br>• Precision@K, Recall@K<br>• Coverage, Diversity<br>• Novelty, Serendipity<br><br><b>Best practice:</b> Cross-validation (5-fold typical)
What are Association Rules and what is Market Basket Analysis?	Association Rules find patterns like "customers who buy X also buy Y" in transaction data.<br><br><b>Market Basket Analysis:</b><br>• Analyzes purchase patterns<br>• Identifies item relationships<br>• Used for cross-selling<br>• Store layout optimization<br><br><b>Format:</b> {Antecedent} → {Consequent}<br>Example: {Bread, Butter} → {Milk}<br><br><b>Applications:</b><br>• Product placement<br>• Bundle recommendations<br>• Promotional strategies<br>• Inventory management
What are Support, Confidence, and Lift in Association Rules?	<b>Support:</b><br>• Frequency of itemset in all transactions<br>• Support(X→Y) = P(X ∪ Y)<br>• Example: 0.05 = appears in 5% of transactions<br>• Higher = more common pattern<br><br><b>Confidence:</b><br>• Strength of rule given antecedent<br>• Confidence(X→Y) = P(Y|X) = Support(X,Y)/Support(X)<br>• Example: 0.75 = 75% with X also have Y<br>• Higher = more reliable rule<br><br><b>Lift:</b><br>• How much X increases likelihood of Y<br>• Lift(X→Y) = Confidence(X→Y)/Support(Y)<br>• > 1: Positive association<br>• = 1: No association<br>• < 1: Negative association
How does the Apriori algorithm work?	Apriori efficiently finds frequent itemsets using the principle: if subset is infrequent, superset is also infrequent.<br><br><b>Algorithm:</b><br>1. Find frequent 1-itemsets (> min_support)<br>2. Generate candidate k-itemsets from (k-1)-itemsets<br>3. Prune using apriori property<br>4. Count support, keep frequent<br>5. Repeat until no frequent itemsets<br>6. Generate rules from frequent itemsets<br>7. Filter by confidence and lift<br><br><b>Parameters:</b><br>• min_support: 0.01-0.1 typical<br>• min_confidence: 0.5-0.7<br>• min_lift: > 1, often > 2
What is the curse of dimensionality and how does it affect clustering?	The curse of dimensionality refers to problems that arise when analyzing high-dimensional data.<br><br><b>Effects on clustering:</b><br>• Distances become meaningless (all points equidistant)<br>• Sparsity increases exponentially<br>• Clusters become less distinct<br>• Computational complexity explodes<br>• Visualization impossible<br><br><b>Solutions:</b><br>• PCA for dimensionality reduction<br>• Feature selection<br>• Use algorithms robust to high-D (DBSCAN)<br>• Increase sample size exponentially<br>• Domain knowledge for feature engineering
When should you standardize/scale your data?	<b>Always standardize for:</b><br>• K-means clustering (uses Euclidean distance)<br>• Hierarchical clustering<br>• PCA (finds max variance directions)<br>• Any distance-based algorithm<br><br><b>Methods:</b><br>• <b>StandardScaler:</b> (x-μ)/σ, normal distribution<br>• <b>MinMaxScaler:</b> Scale to [0,1] range<br>• <b>RobustScaler:</b> Uses median/IQR, handles outliers<br><br><b>Don't scale for:</b><br>• Tree-based methods<br>• Association rules (binary data)<br>• When scale has meaning<br><br><b>Rule:</b> Different scales → must standardize
What is the cophenetic correlation coefficient?	Cophenetic correlation measures how well a dendrogram preserves pairwise distances from original data.<br><br><b>Calculation:</b><br>• Compare original distances vs dendrogram distances<br>• Correlation between these distances<br>• Range: [0, 1]<br><br><b>Interpretation:</b><br>• > 0.7: Good clustering structure<br>• 0.6-0.7: Acceptable<br>• < 0.6: Poor representation<br><br><b>Use for:</b><br>• Comparing linkage methods<br>• Validating hierarchical clustering<br>• Choosing best linkage (highest correlation)
How do you handle missing values for different ML3 algorithms?	<b>For Clustering & PCA:</b><br>• Must handle - no NaN allowed<br>• Numerical: median (robust to outliers)<br>• Categorical: mode or separate category<br>• Never use mean with outliers<br><br><b>For Association Rules:</b><br>• Transaction format: no handling needed<br>• Binary matrix: treat as absent (0)<br><br><b>For Recommendations:</b><br>• Implicit: missing = not interested<br>• Explicit: only use known ratings<br><br><b>Strategies:</b><br>• < 5%: Simple imputation<br>• 5-50%: Advanced imputation<br>• > 50%: Consider dropping feature
What is the difference between hard and soft clustering?	<b>Hard Clustering:</b><br>• Each point belongs to exactly one cluster<br>• Binary membership (0 or 1)<br>• Examples: K-means, Hierarchical, DBSCAN<br>• Output: Cluster labels<br><br><b>Soft Clustering:</b><br>• Points can belong to multiple clusters<br>• Probability/degree of membership<br>• Examples: Fuzzy C-means, GMM<br>• Output: Membership probabilities<br><br><b>When to use soft:</b><br>• Overlapping categories<br>• Uncertainty in assignment<br>• Gradual transitions<br>• Risk assessment
How do you choose between K-means, Hierarchical, and DBSCAN?	<b>Use K-means when:</b><br>• Know K value<br>• Spherical, similar-sized clusters<br>• Large dataset (fast)<br>• Need cluster centers<br><br><b>Use Hierarchical when:</b><br>• Unknown K<br>• Need dendrogram/hierarchy<br>• Small-medium dataset<br>• Various cluster sizes OK<br><br><b>Use DBSCAN when:</b><br>• Arbitrary shapes<br>• Unknown K<br>• Outliers present<br>• Varying densities<br><br><b>Never use:</b><br>• K-means for non-spherical<br>• Hierarchical for huge data<br>• DBSCAN for high dimensions
What are the key hyperparameters for each clustering algorithm?	<b>K-means:</b><br>• n_clusters (K): Number of clusters<br>• n_init: Random initializations (10-20)<br>• max_iter: Convergence limit (300)<br>• random_state: Reproducibility<br><br><b>Hierarchical:</b><br>• n_clusters or distance_threshold<br>• linkage: ward/complete/average/single<br>• metric: distance measure<br><br><b>DBSCAN:</b><br>• eps: Neighborhood radius (critical)<br>• min_samples: Min points for core (≥4)<br>• metric: distance measure<br><br><b>Tuning tips:</b><br>• Grid search with silhouette score<br>• Domain knowledge crucial
What is the elbow method and what are its limitations?	The elbow method plots metric (WCSS/explained variance) vs parameter (K/components) to find optimal value.<br><br><b>How to use:</b><br>1. Calculate metric for range of values<br>2. Plot metric vs parameter<br>3. Look for "elbow" - point where improvement slows<br>4. Choose value at elbow<br><br><b>Limitations:</b><br>• Elbow not always clear<br>• Subjective interpretation<br>• Multiple elbows possible<br>• Smooth curves problematic<br><br><b>Alternatives:</b><br>• Silhouette analysis<br>• Gap statistic<br>• Domain knowledge<br>• Multiple metrics
How do you interpret PCA components?	<b>Interpreting components:</b><br>• Look at loadings (eigenvector values)<br>• High absolute values = important features<br>• Sign indicates direction of relationship<br><br><b>Common patterns:</b><br>• PC1: Overall size/magnitude<br>• PC2: Contrast/shape<br>• PC3+: Specific variations<br><br><b>Example interpretation:</b><br>If PC1 has high positive loadings for all spending variables:<br>"PC1 represents overall spending behavior"<br><br><b>Biplot:</b><br>• Shows samples and variables<br>• Arrows = variable contribution<br>• Angle = correlation between variables
What are the assumptions and limitations of PCA?	<b>Assumptions:</b><br>• Linear relationships between variables<br>• Features are continuous<br>• Larger variance = more important<br>• Normal distribution (ideal)<br><br><b>Limitations:</b><br>• Only captures linear relationships<br>• Sensitive to scaling<br>• Interpretability decreases with components<br>• Outliers affect results<br><br><b>Not suitable for:</b><br>• Categorical data (use MCA)<br>• Non-linear patterns (use t-SNE, UMAP)<br>• When variance ≠ importance<br><br><b>Alternatives:</b><br>• ICA: Independent components<br>• NMF: Non-negative factors<br>• Autoencoders: Non-linear reduction
What is the difference between explicit and implicit feedback in recommendations?	<b>Explicit Feedback:</b><br>• Direct user ratings (1-5 stars)<br>• Clear preference signal<br>• Sparse but reliable<br>• Examples: ratings, likes, reviews<br><br><b>Implicit Feedback:</b><br>• Inferred from behavior<br>• Abundant but noisy<br>• Examples: clicks, views, purchase, time spent<br><br><b>Handling differences:</b><br>• Explicit: Use actual values<br>• Implicit: Convert to confidence scores<br>• Implicit needs different algorithms (ALS)<br>• Negative feedback harder with implicit
What is cold start problem in recommendation systems?	Cold start occurs when insufficient data exists to make good recommendations.<br><br><b>Types:</b><br>• <b>User cold start:</b> New user, no history<br>• <b>Item cold start:</b> New item, no ratings<br>• <b>System cold start:</b> New platform, no data<br><br><b>Solutions:</b><br>• Content-based for item cold start<br>• Demographics for user cold start<br>• Popularity-based fallback<br>• Hybrid approaches<br>• Active learning (ask for ratings)<br>• Transfer learning from similar domains<br><br><b>Best practice:</b> Combine multiple strategies
How do you evaluate clustering quality without ground truth labels?	<b>Internal Metrics:</b><br><br><b>Silhouette Score:</b><br>• Measures cohesion vs separation<br>• Range [-1, 1], higher better<br>• > 0.5 indicates good clustering<br><br><b>Calinski-Harabasz:</b><br>• Ratio of between/within variance<br>• Higher is better<br>• Good for comparing K values<br><br><b>Davies-Bouldin:</b><br>• Average similarity between clusters<br>• Lower is better<br>• Penalizes close clusters<br><br><b>Visual methods:</b><br>• PCA/t-SNE visualization<br>• Cluster profiles<br>• Domain expert validation
What is hierarchical clustering's time and space complexity?	<b>Time Complexity:</b><br>• Standard: O(n³)<br>• With heap: O(n² log n)<br>• Single link: O(n²)<br><br><b>Space Complexity:</b><br>• Distance matrix: O(n²)<br>• Dendrogram storage: O(n)<br><br><b>Implications:</b><br>• Not suitable for n > 10,000<br>• Memory intensive<br>• Use sampling for large datasets<br>• Consider BIRCH for scalability<br><br><b>Comparison:</b><br>• K-means: O(n·k·d·i) - much faster<br>• DBSCAN: O(n log n) with index
How do you handle outliers in different ML3 algorithms?	<b>K-means:</b><br>• Very sensitive - outliers become centroids<br>• Remove using IQR/Z-score first<br>• Or use K-medoids (more robust)<br><br><b>Hierarchical:</b><br>• Complete linkage: robust<br>• Single linkage: very sensitive<br>• Ward: moderately sensitive<br><br><b>DBSCAN:</b><br>• Automatically identifies as noise<br>• No preprocessing needed<br>• Tune eps/min_samples<br><br><b>PCA:</b><br>• Sensitive - affects variance<br>• Use RobustPCA or remove first<br><br><b>Recommendations:</b><br>• Cap extreme ratings<br>• Use robust loss functions
What are the key differences between Euclidean and Manhattan distance?	<b>Euclidean Distance:</b><br>• Straight-line distance<br>• √(Σ(xi - yi)²)<br>• Sensitive to outliers (squared terms)<br>• Most common in clustering<br><br><b>Manhattan Distance:</b><br>• Sum of absolute differences<br>• Σ|xi - yi|<br>• More robust to outliers<br>• Better for high dimensions<br>• Grid-like movement<br><br><b>When to use:</b><br>• Euclidean: continuous, low-D<br>• Manhattan: discrete, high-D, outliers<br>• Cosine: text, high-D sparse
How do you determine optimal eps for DBSCAN?	<b>K-distance Graph Method:</b><br>1. Calculate k-nearest neighbor distances<br>2. Sort distances in ascending order<br>3. Plot k-distance graph<br>4. Look for "knee" in curve<br>5. Eps = distance at knee<br><br><b>Guidelines:</b><br>• k = minPts (typically 4-5)<br>• k = 2*dimensions for high-D<br>• Sharp increase = natural boundary<br><br><b>Alternative approaches:</b><br>• Domain knowledge<br>• Try multiple values<br>• Use OPTICS (automatic eps)<br>• Statistical methods (mean + 2*std)
What is the difference between batch and incremental learning for clustering?	<b>Batch Learning:</b><br>• Process all data at once<br>• Standard K-means, hierarchical<br>• Needs all data in memory<br>• Can't adapt to new data<br><br><b>Incremental Learning:</b><br>• Process data in chunks/streams<br>• Mini-batch K-means, BIRCH<br>• Memory efficient<br>• Adapts to new data<br><br><b>When to use incremental:</b><br>• Data doesn't fit in memory<br>• Streaming data<br>• Concept drift expected<br>• Online learning needed<br><br><b>Trade-offs:</b><br>• Batch: Better quality<br>• Incremental: Scalability, adaptability
What are latent factors in matrix factorization?	Latent factors are hidden features learned by decomposing user-item matrix.<br><br><b>Interpretation:</b><br>• Capture abstract preferences<br>• Not directly observable<br>• Examples: "likes action", "prefers classics"<br><br><b>How many factors?</b><br>• Typically 50-200<br>• More factors = better fit but overfitting risk<br>• Cross-validate to choose<br><br><b>Benefits:</b><br>• Compress sparse matrix<br>• Generalize beyond observed<br>• Handle missing values<br>• Interpretable patterns<br><br><b>Regularization:</b> Prevents overfitting factors
How do you handle categorical variables in clustering?	<b>Encoding Methods:</b><br><br><b>One-Hot Encoding:</b><br>• Creates binary columns<br>• Good for nominal categories<br>• Increases dimensionality<br><br><b>Label Encoding:</b><br>• Numbers for categories<br>• Only for ordinal data<br>• Implies false order<br><br><b>Target Encoding:</b><br>• Use target statistics<br>• Risk of overfitting<br><br><b>Best Practices:</b><br>• Separate clustering for categorical<br>• Use Gower distance (mixed types)<br>• K-modes for categorical only<br>• PCA after one-hot encoding
What is cluster validation and what are the main approaches?	<b>Three main approaches:</b><br><br><b>1. External Validation:</b><br>• Compare to known labels<br>• Metrics: Rand Index, NMI<br>• Not always available<br><br><b>2. Internal Validation:</b><br>• Use data characteristics<br>• Silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz<br>• No ground truth needed<br><br><b>3. Relative Validation:</b><br>• Compare different parameters<br>• Find best K, linkage, etc.<br>• Use multiple metrics<br><br><b>Stability Analysis:</b><br>• Cluster on subsamples<br>• Consistent results = stable<br>• Bootstrap validation
When should you use cosine similarity vs Euclidean distance?	<b>Cosine Similarity:</b><br>• Measures angle between vectors<br>• Ignores magnitude<br>• Range: [-1, 1]<br>• Use for: text, high-D sparse, ratings<br><br><b>Euclidean Distance:</b><br>• Measures absolute distance<br>• Considers magnitude<br>• Range: [0, ∞)<br>• Use for: continuous, dense, spatial<br><br><b>Example:</b><br>• User rates 5,5,5 vs 1,1,1<br>• Euclidean: very different<br>• Cosine: same (both consistent)<br><br><b>Rule:</b> Direction matters → Cosine<br>Magnitude matters → Euclidean
What are the practical considerations when implementing PCA?	<b>Before PCA:</b><br>• Must standardize data<br>• Handle missing values<br>• Check for outliers<br>• Ensure numerical data<br><br><b>During PCA:</b><br>• Center data (subtract mean)<br>• Choose solver (full, randomized)<br>• Set random_state<br><br><b>After PCA:</b><br>• Can't interpret features directly<br>• Save PCA object for new data<br>• Transform test same as train<br><br><b>Common mistakes:</b><br>• Forgetting to scale<br>• PCA on train+test together<br>• Using too few components
How do you implement a recommendation system for a new product with no ratings?	<b>Strategies for new items:</b><br><br><b>1. Content-Based Features:</b><br>• Use item metadata<br>• Find similar existing items<br>• Transfer ratings from similar<br><br><b>2. Popularity-Based:</b><br>• Recommend to active users<br>• Boost in trending sections<br><br><b>3. Demographic Matching:</b><br>• Target likely audience<br>• Use user segments<br><br><b>4. Exploration Strategy:</b><br>• Random exposure to diverse users<br>• Collect initial feedback<br>• Multi-armed bandit approach<br><br><b>Best:</b> Hybrid with gradual transition to CF
What is the difference between density-based and partition-based clustering?	<b>Partition-Based (K-means):</b><br>• Divides into K partitions<br>• Assumes spherical clusters<br>• Every point assigned<br>• Minimizes within-cluster variance<br><br><b>Density-Based (DBSCAN):</b><br>• Groups by density<br>• Arbitrary shapes<br>• Can mark outliers<br>• Based on neighborhood density<br><br><b>Key differences:</b><br>• Shape flexibility<br>• Outlier handling<br>• Need for K<br>• Computational approach<br><br><b>Choose density when:</b> Unknown shapes/K, outliers present
How do you combine PCA with clustering effectively?	<b>Workflow:</b><br>1. Standardize data<br>2. Apply PCA<br>3. Keep 90% variance<br>4. Cluster on PC scores<br>5. Interpret in original space<br><br><b>Benefits:</b><br>• Reduces noise<br>• Speeds up clustering<br>• Avoids curse of dimensionality<br>• Better visualization<br><br><b>Considerations:</b><br>• May lose important local structure<br>• Linear assumptions<br>• Validate against original features<br><br><b>Alternative:</b> Cluster then PCA for visualization
What are the key parameters for SVD in surprise library?	<b>Main parameters:</b><br><br><b>n_factors:</b><br>• Number of latent factors<br>• Default: 100<br>• Typical: 50-200<br>• More = better fit, slower<br><br><b>n_epochs:</b><br>• Training iterations<br>• Default: 20<br>• Increase if not converged<br><br><b>lr_all:</b><br>• Learning rate<br>• Default: 0.005<br>• Lower if unstable<br><br><b>reg_all:</b><br>• Regularization<br>• Default: 0.02<br>• Higher to prevent overfit<br><br><b>Best practice:</b> Grid search with cross-validation
How do you interpret association rules with high lift but low support?	<b>High Lift, Low Support means:</b><br>• Strong association when it occurs<br>• But occurs rarely<br>• Niche pattern<br><br><b>Examples:</b><br>• Luxury items bought together<br>• Specialized products<br>• Seasonal combinations<br><br><b>Business value:</b><br>• High margin opportunities<br>• Targeted marketing<br>• Inventory for specific segments<br><br><b>Caution:</b><br>• May not be statistically significant<br>• Verify with confidence<br>• Consider minimum transaction threshold<br><br><b>Rule:</b> Balance lift, support, and business relevance
What is the minimum support threshold and how do you choose it?	<b>Minimum support filters infrequent itemsets.</b><br><br><b>Choosing threshold:</b><br>• Dense data: 0.1-0.5<br>• Sparse data: 0.01-0.1<br>• Rare items: 0.001-0.01<br><br><b>Too high:</b><br>• Miss interesting patterns<br>• Only obvious rules<br><br><b>Too low:</b><br>• Computational explosion<br>• Many spurious rules<br>• Statistical noise<br><br><b>Strategy:</b><br>• Start at 0.1<br>• Adjust based on results<br>• Consider business minimum<br>• Use lift to filter further
